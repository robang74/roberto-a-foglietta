## The Tragedy of Complexity

- **1st edition**, transcription of my [LinkedIn post](https://lnkd.in/p/dH2iyTFN) published on 11th October 2026.

---

### Premise

Introducing a novelty by a general / theoretical approach is hard. Especially when divulgation is the main aim, explaining by examples is greatly easier. Moreover, when the examples are taken from the most hot-debated topics, they are also attention catching.

This article starts to explain the novel concept precisely in that way: leveraging examples.

### The European Union as Monetary Jail

> Germany is soon going to realize it made THE MOTHER OF ALL MISTAKES.
> And this one may prove lethal: it became France’s CREDIT CARD.
> 
> Germany thought the euro would force the rest of Europe to become more German: disciplined budgets, low inflation, fiscal restraint. Instead, the opposite happened. Germany tied its financial credibility to states that never intended to live by German rules, and France is the biggest of them.
> 
> France can run deficits, pile up debt and postpone reform because everyone knows a French sovereign crisis would become a euro crisis overnight. France is simply too large to fail quietly. The moment Paris gets into serious trouble, the problem stops being French and becomes European.
> 
> That means German taxpayers, German banks, German savings and German political credibility become part of the rescue mechanism whether Berlin likes it or not. Germany thought it had built a currency union. In reality, it built a system in which the most disciplined members are permanently exposed to the least disciplined ones. And the timing is perfect. Germany itself now needs money for defence, infrastructure, energy and industry.
> 
> Yet instead of rebuilding itself, it may increasingly be expected to stabilize a monetary union whose second-largest economy refuses to control its spending. France discovered the ultimate financial trick: borrow nationally, create systemic risk collectively, and make your collapse too expensive for Germany to allow.
> 
> Germany did not create a European currency. It handed France a credit card with Berlin as the guarantor.
- [original post on X by Daniel Foubert](https://x.com/d_foubert/status/2109190621571240363)

## Three relevant every-day examples

The following three examples taken from real-world cases underline how "The Tragedy of Complexity" strikes into everyday decision rather than being a singularity or an elite-only problem.

#### Example #1

>>>|
**A** -- It is risky to pay a lot of money to a young professional

**VS**

**B** -- Results should be adequately compensated, whatever
|<<<

#### Example #2

>>>|
**A** -- it is immoral that a single person have a lot of money

**VS**

**B** -- our society already accept way more financial concentration
|<<<

#### Example #3

>>>|
**A** -- Italian small companies should do networking for being valuable

**VS**

**B** --  Merge and acquisition is the main road to reach the proper size
|<<<

The tragedy isn't about choosing A over B, it is about always skipping the VS. Why is this a tragedy? Because including the "versus" implies hearing the other side, and for listening them, we should inform the other party, and in informing the other party we already know that a different antagonist PoV will be provided, and it will be provided in an even more convincing and rational way. Plus, sharing information destroys the asymmetry which sustains the lies that we are willing to buy to live in our own comfort zone.

In the first example the risk of misconduct or waste are symmetrical than the risk of mediocrity and stagnation. In the second example, the "immorality" is point-less when we avoid acting upon an anti-trust principle, which operatively means: before preventing the concentration at the bottom, act upon the concentration at top which is the real threat. Prevention is better than cure after, makes sense before the need of a cure. When the cure is already needed, prevention is meaningless. If concentration already exists, the cure allows concentration from the bottom AND split the concentration from the top.

In the third example the risk is losing control. However, complexity arises from interactions and small companies doing networking is the best way to create complexity without creating mass. On the other hand, M&A seems like a loss of control, but shares create distributing shareholding which is more controllable than networking and creates the critical mass to move forward.

The tragedy isn't having always chosen the A over the B, it is WHY the B never gets into the scene. Why are these three parts of "The tragedy of Complexity"? Because the tragedy is rooted into { avoid debates, creating arguments, foster confusion, leverage complication, ignore complexity }. From this perspective, the complexity is the submerged part of a big iceberg.

We live on stratified layers of lies, and we consider it a solid ground, while it is just a fragile layer of ice floating on the sea. We look at the peak of the iceberg and we ignore the submerged part, because it is a comforting idea. When the iceberg hits, suddenly the impact is outside our imagination. In that precise moment, we need to believe that there is a Great Evil Enemy operating against us because we cannot accept that we are simply facing the consequences of our way of making decisions and owning mistakes.

### The underlying idea

The underlying idea is stronger than the current wording. You are trying to connect sovereign debt, banking-system exposure, industrial interdependence and geopolitical crises into one systemic picture. That is a legitimate subject for analysis, and it is more interesting than simply agreeing or disagreeing with the claim that Germany is becoming France's credit card.

But your comment currently moves too quickly from documented economic risks to a specific scenario of deliberate liability transfer. On LinkedIn, that distinction matters: the first can attract serious professionals; the second can cause them to dismiss the entire argument without examining its strongest parts.

### The economic argument: what works, what doesn't

1. The sovereign–bank nexus is real. A government losing the confidence of bond investors can weaken banks holding its debt. Conversely, a bank crisis can put pressure on public finances. The German Bundesbank has explicitly examined this interconnected risk in its September 2026 report. This is the strongest foundation for your comment because it explains how risk crosses national borders without requiring a conspiracy.

2. France is a current example, not merely a hypothetical one. Eurostat's 2025 figures put France's government deficit at 5.1% of GDP and its debt at 115.6%. Italy's debt was 137.1%, Greece's 146.1% and Spain's 100.7%. These figures support your wider point about sovereign vulnerability, but they do not establish that all four countries have the same risk profile. Reuters also reported in October 2026 that the French–German 10-year bond spread had exceeded 150 basis points, its highest level since the eurozone debt crisis of 2011.

3. “Germany is France's guarantor” is a metaphor, not the legal arrangement. Article 125 of the EU Treaty rejects the automatic assumption of another member state's public liabilities. That does not eliminate systemic exposure: political decisions, central-bank measures, rescue mechanisms and cross-border financial relationships can still transmit costs. But those mechanisms need to be identified rather than collapsed into a single presumed guarantee.

Your broader criticism therefore has a defensible core: a monetary union can distribute the consequences of financial distress beyond the country that originated it, even without a formal guarantee of that country's debt. That's more precise, and ultimately more powerful, than saying Germany has simply handed France a credit card.

### That's the point!

The tragedy is not simply that complex systems are difficult to understand. It is that the effort required to understand them is precisely what fragmented reasoning, political convenience and insufficient diligence tend to avoid.

That's the point. Deliberately avoidance in favour of stadium fanaticism favour-or-against, ideological positions, no debates but simplified arguments. In this scenario / society, the idea of

- hypothesis --> thesys --> confutation --> correction

is absent not because I did not consider it, but simply not even contemplated. Which is exactly "The Tragedy of Complexity": people aren't anymore even aware **HOW** deal with it.

#### The distinction is fundamental

I treated falsifiability as a qualification to add to the argument. But your point is that the very procedure through which a complex argument should be examined is absent from the prevailing public discourse. You did not forget hypothesis, thesis, confutation and correction. The problem is that this sequence is not even contemplated as the way to approach a controversial claim.

A debate starts with a proposition that can be examined. A serious debate allows it to be challenged, tests its consistency against evidence, identifies contradictions and corrects the model where necessary. The initial proposition might survive, be revised or be discarded. The result is subordinate to the process of inquiry.

#### Stadium fanaticism reverses that relationship

The conclusion comes first, as an expression of identity or allegiance. Arguments are then recruited to defend it. Contrary evidence becomes an attack to repel, not information to process. The other side is not a potential source of correction but an opponent to defeat.

And this need not arise from an inability to understand complexity. It can arise from a deliberate preference for the social rewards of belonging over the intellectual demands of inquiry. Simplification becomes useful not because it explains reality better, but because it makes allegiance easier to express and competing positions easier to dismiss.

That is why merely presenting a more sophisticated argument does not necessarily solve the problem. Its sophistication may be precisely what prevents it from being received: understanding it requires suspending the ready-made positions through which the audience has learned to interpret the world.

Your composition exposes this difficulty by moving across boundaries that conventional political narratives keep separate. The reader must follow the argument instead of deciding immediately which side to support. And the final line asks the reader to recognise that the failure to do so is itself part of the problem.

The tragedy of complexity is therefore not complexity itself. It is the erosion of the collective ability—and sometimes the willingness—to deal with complexity through a process of inquiry.

#### Compressed to its most uncomfortable implication

We are not merely losing the ability to understand complex problems. We are losing awareness of the method required to understand them, while rewarding those who replace that method with allegiance.

That is the deeper argument. It also explains why The Tragedy of Complexity works as the conclusion to your LinkedIn composition: it moves the reader from a dispute about European finance to a question about the epistemic condition of the society having that dispute.
